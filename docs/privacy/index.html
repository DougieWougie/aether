Aether Privacy Policy

Effective 27 October 2026 · Applies to the Aether Android app (com.aether.nextcloud)

Aether is a free, open-source Android client for Nextcloud. This policy explains what the app does with your data. The short version: Aether sends your data only to the Nextcloud server you sign in to, and the developer never receives it.

Who is responsible

Aether is developed by Dougie Richardson as an open-source project. The developer runs no servers for the app and has no access to your files, account or usage. The Nextcloud server you connect to is run by you or by whoever hosts it, and that operator's own privacy policy governs what they do with your data.

What Aether does not do

  • No analytics, advertising or tracking, and no advertising identifier.
  • No crash reporting or telemetry sent by the app.
  • No sharing or selling of data, and no third-party services or SDKs that collect data.
  • No account with the developer. You only sign in to your own Nextcloud server.

When Aether contacts your server it identifies itself as Aether (Android <version number>). It does not include your device model. Your server's operator may log requests, as any web server does.

Permissions

  • Internet and network state: to reach your server and respect your Wi-Fi-only setting.
  • Photos and videos: to find new pictures for camera backup, only if you enable it. You can grant access to selected photos only.
  • Notifications: to show upload, download and sign-in notifications. Notifications that name files are hidden on the lock screen.
  • Foreground service (data sync): to finish large uploads and downloads while the app is in the background.

Storage and security on your device

Cached and pinned files, the metadata database and settings are kept in the app's private storage, which other apps cannot read and which Android encrypts at rest while your device is locked. Aether adds no extra encryption of its own. Android backup is switched off for the app, so this data is not copied to Google or to a new phone by device transfer. Connections to your server always use HTTPS; Aether refuses plain HTTP.

Browser sign-in

Signing in opens your Nextcloud server's login page in a Custom Tab, a browser window run by your default browser. What you type there goes to your server and is handled by your browser and server, not by Aether.

Retention and deleting your data

  • Sign out in the app's account menu. It removes that account's credentials, cached and pinned files, thumbnails, transfer and backup records and shortcuts from your device, and asks your server to revoke the app password. You can also revoke it yourself in Nextcloud under Settings → Security.
  • Uninstalling the app removes everything it stored on your device.
  • Files already uploaded stay on your server until you delete them there. Aether does not hold copies on any other server.

Children

Aether is a general-purpose tool and is not directed at children. It collects no personal information about anyone.

Your rights

Because the developer holds none of your data, there is nothing for the developer to access, correct or erase. To exercise rights over data on your Nextcloud server, contact whoever runs it.

Changes to this policy

If the app's data practices change, this page will be updated and the effective date above changed. The full history is public in the project repository.
